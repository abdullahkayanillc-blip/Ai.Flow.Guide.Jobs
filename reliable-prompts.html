How to write a reliable task prompt

Good prompts remove ambiguity. They do not need theatrical wording, twelve emojis, or mystical declarations that the model is “the world's greatest expert.”

The six-part structure

1. Role

Define the relevant perspective, such as customer-support specialist, editor, analyst, or technical writer.

2. Task

State exactly what should be produced. Use a concrete verb: classify, summarize, compare, draft, extract, calculate.

3. Audience

Identify who will read or use the output. This changes vocabulary, detail, and tone.

4. Context

Supply the facts the model needs. Separate known information from assumptions.

5. Constraints

Set length, format, prohibited content, required fields, and any business rules.

6. Output format

Tell the model what a successful answer looks like: table, JSON, email, checklist, headings, or another clear structure.

Verification matters

For important work, instruct the model to state uncertainty and avoid inventing missing facts. Then validate critical fields in your application or automation rather than trusting generated text blindly.

Reliable AI use is less “find the secret prompt” and more “design a clear interface for the task.”
